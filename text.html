Identifying AI-Generated Text

AI Detectors

While AI detection tools such as ZeroGPT and Pangram are useful for getting an idea as to whether a piece of writing is AI-generated, they should not be relied upon solely because they are prone to errors. This can be tested easily by entering a very old piece of writing (such as the Declaration of Independence). The AI detector will likely report this text as being AI-generated. On the other hand, text that is AI-generated can be missed by AI detecors. It is even possible to ask AI to rephrase the text in order to avoid detection by AI detectors.

AI detectors work by detecting patterns that would be seen in AI-generated text. At their simplest, AI language models work by predicting which word is most likely to come after the previous one by analysing millions of words. This makes AI-generated text more predictable than human written text, which AI detectors leverage to determine whether a piece of text is AI-generated. While this sounds like it should work well, AI detectors are very rarely accurate and should not be treated as a consensus on whether a piece of writing is AI-generated.

Signs of AI Writing

Content

LLMs struggle to keep a neutral tone when describing a topic, instead often swaying towards promotional or advertisement-like writing. AI writing will also often emphasise the significance and legacy of a subject, even when it is actually quite mundane. They also often attribute opinions to vague authority rather than any specific body or organisation. Another sign of AI writing is the use of "-ing" words to create superficial analysis at the end of sentences.

Language and Grammar

Certain words are overused in AI writing. A famous example of this is the word "delve", which was overused by ChatGPT in 2023 and 2024, but is not seen as much now. Overused words in modern ChatGPT include "emphasizing", "enhance", "highlighting" and "showcasing". AI also has the tendency to avoid simple sentence constructions using "is", "are" or "was", instead using phrases like "serves as". AI also tends describe things as though it were clearing a common misconception, using phrases such as "not (just) X, but (also) Y" or "X rather than Y". Another sign of AI writing is the overuse of the rule of three, listing three examples of something or using three adjectives to describe something.

Style

AI-generated writing notoriously overuse em dashes (—), while humans would rarely bother to use this character as it is difficult to type. Similarly, they use curly quotation marks (“...” or ‘...’) rather than straight quotes. These symbols are also difficult to type, and as such are rare in human writing. It is important to note that certain word processors such as Google Docs automatically change multiple hyphens (--) into em dashes and straight quotes into curly quotes.

Words Intended for the User

An obvious giveaway of AI writing is if the person forgets to remove communication that was intended for the user of the AI. Phrases like "I hope this helps", "You're absolutely right!" or "Is there anything else" is the LLM communicating with the person using AI to write the text, and sometimes people are stupid enough to leave that in.
