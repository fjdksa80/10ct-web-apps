Identifying AI-Generated Images

SynthID

SynthID is a watermarking technology developed by Google which embeds invisible signals into AI-generated content. Because these signals are a part of the content themselves, unlike metadata they are resistant to modifications like cropping, filters, compression and screenshotting. You can use SynthID to investigate whether an image is generated by AI by asking Google Gemini if the image contains SynthID watermarks.

Content Credentials

The Coalition for Content Provenance and Authenticity (C2PA) is a technical standard which defines how digital content can carry a record of where it came from, who made it and how it has changed. Unlike traditional metadata, C2PA binds metadata to the content using cryptographic signatures, making tampering detectable. Content Credentials is the user-facing implementation of the C2PA standard which allows people to see the origin of digital content.

This tool by OpenAI uses both SynthID and Content Credentials to verify whether an image or audio file was generated with OpenAI.

Analysing Image Details

AI-generated images often look realistic at a glance, but when you zoom in and scrutinise the finer details, you will notice things start to break down.

Hands

AI is notoriously bad at creating hands. While they have gotten better over the years, this is still a useful identifier for AI-generated images, especially if the hands are not the main focus of the image, but rather a smaller detail. In AI-generated images, you might see hands with extra fingers, missing fingers or fingers that are warped and twisted in strange ways.

Text

AI also used to be notoriously bad with text, but has since gotten quite good, being able to generate perfectly normal-looking text. Like with hands, this often breaks down at the small scale, where text isn't the main focus. When an image model tries to generate text, it will often make spelling mistakes and at its worst, will be completely illegible, comprising of shapes that vaguely look like writing.

Repeating Patterns and Textures

In AI-generated images, you will often see repeating patterns where you would expect chaos, in places like hair, leaves or wood. Inversely, AI-generated images will often have chaos where you would expect a repeating pattern. Typically, this occurs when the pattern repeats at a smaller scale.

Broken Lines

When a line (tree, pipe, windowsill, etc.) in an AI-generated image becomes obfuscated by an object, it will sometimes not come out the other side when you would expect it to, seemingly disappearing or ending behind the object.

Lights and Shadows

AI-generated images will often have highlights on a side of the object not facing a light source. This is especially obvious when the image only has one clear light source, for example, a scene that is outside where the only light source is the sun. They will also often have shadows facing towards a light source. Another sign is reflections that look strange or don't make sense.

Relationships between Objects

While an AI might be able to create a convincing image of an object, when many objects are placed within a scene, their composition, placement or interactions might not make sense. Items might seem out of place or with other items that it shouldn't typically be with.
